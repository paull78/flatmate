# Flatmate's philosophy

Flatmate is a home planner for people with a real home to plan: a tiny house, a flat, a small house with a loft, and later a home on several floors. You draw it as a plan, furnish it, and see it in 3D, alone or together with others and with Claude.

## Plan first, 2.5D always

Every part of a home in Flatmate is a shape on a plan with a height range. A wall is its footprint from the floor to the ceiling. A loft is a rectangle at 2.15 m. A bed is its outline and how tall it stands. The only slopes are the roof and the pitch of a stair. That is what we call 2.5D, and it is a choice, not a limit we are waiting to remove.

Free 3D modelling asks people to think in meshes, faces and cameras. Homes don't need it: almost everything in a home stands on a floor and has a height. Drawing on the plan keeps every gesture simple, keeps the rules exact (a desk fits under a loft bed because their heights don't meet), and still gives a true 3D view of the result. When something can't be drawn as a shape with a height, Flatmate draws its nearest 2.5D form or leaves it out.

## The principles, in order

When two of them pull in different directions, the one higher in the list wins.

**1. The drawing is always true.** Every edit leaves a drawing that makes sense: walls that join, furniture that fits, stairs that reach where they go. What you see while you drag is exactly what you get when you let go, and everyone editing the same drawing sees the same thing.

**2. 2.5D is the boundary.** A plan shape and a height range, nothing more. The 3D view shows the home; the plan is where you change it.

**3. Usable without a manual.** Point to say what you want, type a number to say exactly how much. When Flatmate refuses an edit, it says why, where you are looking. Rules about comfort, like the room you need in front of a wardrobe, warn instead of refusing. Every gesture is one undo. Every control shows its choices.

**4. Right the first time.** A piece you place should need no fixing: a stair drawn next to a wall turns its entrance away from it, a low step has no handrails, a fresh drawing has no warnings.

**5. Real homes set the scope.** A feature earns its place when real tiny houses, flats and small homes need it, and the more of them need it, the sooner it comes. One house's quirk waits.

**6. Grow by reuse, not by new concepts.** A new option or size on something that exists beats a new tool, mode or panel. Every new concept is one more thing to learn, so it must replace more than it adds. When in doubt, Flatmate leaves a feature out or keeps it simple with a clear limit.

**7. Delight comes from care.** Plans drawn the way architects draw them. Things that look like what they are: a drawer has a handle, a winder stair has its turning treads. Feedback the moment you move. Nothing decorative for its own sake.

**8. Claude is a collaborator.** Whatever you can do in a drawing, Claude can do too, through the same rules, and you watch it happen live.

## What Flatmate isn't

It isn't a 3D modeller: you can't sculpt a roof or bend a wall in space. It isn't a construction tool either: its stair and guard rules are rules of thumb that warn, never a compliance check. And it doesn't try to do everything: each feature has to earn its place by principles 5 and 6.
